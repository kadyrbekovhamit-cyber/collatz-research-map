# GERO: Collatz in colour

Audience: mathematically curious viewers, graph-visualization practitioners and potential independent reviewers. Goal: help people inspect a public research record and submit specific corrections.

Format: original English narrated 3D tour, approximately 2–3 minutes, 1280×720, 24 fps. Real rotation of the published dependency graph, with a historical timeline and selected obligations. Matching accessible interactive Canvas page with search, category filters and click-to-inspect labels. No autoplay or third-party trackers.

Main claim: a transparent research map can separate internally checked conditional arguments, corrections and unresolved obligations. It is not a proof of the Collatz conjecture.

Palette inspired by vivid molecular visualizations. No AlphaFold assets, logos, confidence scores or scientific equivalence. Blue = internally checked; cyan = selected published references; coral = documented correction; amber = unresolved. Text labels and shapes supplement colour.

Authors: Khamit Kadyrbekov and Daniyal Kadirbekov. Synthetic narration: Jenny through edge-tts. AI-assisted research, drafting, internal checks and production disclosed. No named historical mathematician is presented as a collaborator.

Review request: identify a node/report, a missing assumption, a calculation error or a relevant earlier theorem. No requests to upvote or coordinated engagement.
